Hormonal regulation of blood glucose (insulin and glucagon)

Blood glucose is held in a narrow range (about 70-110 mg/dL when fasting) by two
opposing pancreatic hormones secreted by the islets of Langerhans. Beta cells
secrete insulin; alpha cells secrete glucagon. The two form a negative-feedback
loop around a set point.

Insulin is released when blood glucose is high (for example after a meal). It is
an anabolic, glucose-lowering signal. It promotes glucose uptake into skeletal
muscle and adipose tissue by triggering translocation of GLUT4 transporters to
the cell membrane, stimulates glycogenesis (glucose stored as glycogen in liver
and muscle), promotes lipogenesis, and suppresses gluconeogenesis. The net effect
is to move glucose out of the blood and into storage.

Glucagon is released when blood glucose is low (for example during fasting or
exercise). It is a catabolic, glucose-raising signal that acts mainly on the
liver. It stimulates glycogenolysis (breakdown of stored glycogen to release
glucose) and gluconeogenesis (synthesis of new glucose from lactate, glycerol,
and glucogenic amino acids). The net effect is to raise blood glucose.

Mechanism: glucagon binds a Gs-protein-coupled receptor on hepatocytes, which
activates adenylate cyclase, raising cyclic AMP (cAMP), which activates protein
kinase A (PKA). PKA phosphorylates target enzymes, activating glycogen
phosphorylase (favoring glycogen breakdown) and inhibiting glycogen synthase.
Insulin signaling opposes this through a receptor tyrosine kinase cascade.

Because the two hormones oppose each other around a set point, what matters
physiologically is often the insulin-to-glucagon ratio rather than either level
alone: a high ratio after a meal favors storage, a low ratio during fasting
favors mobilization. Epinephrine reinforces glucagon's effects during acute
stress ("fight or flight"). In type 1 diabetes the loss of beta cells removes
insulin, so glucose cannot be cleared and the counter-regulatory glucagon signal
is left unopposed, driving hyperglycemia.
